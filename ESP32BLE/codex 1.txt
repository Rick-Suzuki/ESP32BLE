B. BRIGHT VERSUS DARK COMPARISON

Confirmed by source:

Bright viewer-owned lines:
• Use opaque filled rectangles.
• Are rendered entirely within their assigned 1-point frame.
• Are not followed by a local clipping modifier.

Dark lower-panel lines:
• Use centered shape strokes.
• Are followed by clipShape after the overlay.
• The top and bottom portions of the centered stroke lie at the view boundary and are subject to clipping.

This is a confirmed construction difference: fill versus centered stroke followed by clipping.

The Templates table uses the same centered-stroke-plus-clip construction as the lower Settings panel:
• Set_Table.swift:260–271.

Therefore, source alone predicts that the table outer edges and lower-panel outer edges should share similar rendering characteristics.

Not confirmed by source:

• A separate additional bright line at the current viewer/table boundary.
• Any opacity difference between the viewer, table, or lower-panel white borders.
• Any current duplicate line at viewer/table or viewer/lower-panel boundaries.

The current source has only one owner at each shared boundary.

Ruled out by source:

• Different declared white opacity: all relevant borders use Color.white without opacity reduction.
• Different declared nominal width: all relevant borders are 1 point.
• A remaining Templates viewer right-edge or bottom-edge border: neither exists after Stage 8B.24C.2.
• A Templates table description section adding a second border on iPad: that section is limited to iPhone at Set_Table.swift:382–391.

C. CLIPPING ANALYSIS

Supported by source:

A centered 1-point stroke traces the Rectangle outline. The inner-stroke API, strokeBorder, differs specifically by insetting before stroking. The current table and lower panel use stroke, not strokeBorder.

The lower panel applies .clipShape after its stroke overlay:
• Set_Screen.swift:3653–3657.

The table applies .clipShape after its stroke overlay:
• Set_Table.swift:260–271.

Thus, the outer half of a centered stroke can be excluded at the component boundary. This is a source-supported explanation for a line that appears thinner or darker than the viewer’s full in-bounds fill.

The viewer top, left, and center divider use fills rather than strokes and have no corresponding local clip. They are not subject to the same construction.

The ContentView clip at ContentView.swift:335–336 is additionally relevant to the visible bottom-most Settings line, because the lower Settings panel can coincide with the Settings container boundary.

F. ROOT CAUSE

Confirmed cause:

The interface mixes two materially different line-construction paths:

1. Viewer-owned boundaries:
Opaque 1-point filled rectangles inside their view bounds.

2. Table and lower-panel boundaries:
Centered 1-point Rectangle strokes followed by clipping.

This difference alone is sufficient to produce different apparent brightness or thickness, especially at outer edges.

Likely contributing cause:

Fractional point positions can cause antialiasing variation:
• The 64% divider is explicitly fractional on many widths.
• The table edge can be fractional due to ratio-based width calculation.
• The vertical allocation boundary is runtime-dependent.

Unverified cause:

The exact extent to which each physical iPad’s renderer makes a specific line brighter or darker cannot be proven from static source alone. The reported bright table boundary is not explained by a second line in the current source; it requires physical-device frame/pixel inspection to correlate the screenshot marking with the active line.

G. RECOMMENDED CORRECTION

Do not make another Templates-only cosmetic adjustment.

The evidence-supported future correction, if physical measurement confirms the appearance remains unacceptable, is a separate shared iPad Settings border-standardization stage:

• Change the table outer border and lower-panel outer border from centered stroke plus clipping to a fully in-bounds 1-point inner border, such as strokeBorder.
• Preserve one owner per shared boundary.
• Leave the Templates viewer’s existing in-bounds filled lines unchanged.
• Verify all iPad Settings categories because SettingsFileTable and combinedBottomPanelSection are shared.

Before that shared change, perform a narrow physical-device measurement to capture:
• display scale;
• viewer frame;
• contentsWidth;
• table frame;
• lower-panel frame;
• Settings container frame.

This avoids another speculative correction.

I. REGRESSION RISKS

A Templates-only change cannot make the table and lower-panel strokes identical without either adding a duplicate line or changing their shared implementations.

Changing SettingsFileTable would affect iPad table borders for Screens, Text, Markdown, Images, Sounds, PDFs, and Templates.

Changing combinedBottomPanelSection would affect the lower Settings panel in all iPad categories.

No iPhone impact is required if a future shared correction is explicitly guarded for iPad, but that must be verified in a dedicated implementation stage.
