B. iPad Image opacity:

C–F. Key Down and Key Gap on iPhone and iPad:
• Shared implementation, not device-specific: Set_KB_Timing.swift:61-72 and 116-165.
• Both native Sliders use .tint(.white).
• Both inherit .disabled(!isTimingControlsEnabled).
• Their common parent also applies .opacity(0.45).

VERIFIED CAUSE

The timing sliders have a materially different hierarchy from the Image slider:

Image slider:
• disabled environment
• 45%-white tint
• no parent opacity

Timing slider:
• disabled environment
• white tint
• parent opacity of 45%

The parent opacity attenuates the entire native slider after its platform rendering, including an already-dark unfilled track. This explains the weak tracks and contributes to dark thumbs. It also affects the ON/OFF labels, timing values, and iPad arrows.

The source has no iPhone/iPad-specific timing-slider wrapper. Any remaining device difference is native Slider rendering and cannot be proven further from source alone.

SMALLEST SAFE CORRECTION

In Set_KB_Timing.swift:

1. Keep the existing parent .disabled modifier.
2. Remove only the parent .opacity(0.45).
3. Apply opacity 0.45 individually to the existing ON/OFF labels, timing-value labels, and iPad arrows, preserving their present appearance.
4. Give each timing Slider the same conditional tint chain as the Image slider:
   • enabled: .white
   • disabled: SettingsImageControlAppearance.disabledColor
5. Do not add a Slider-specific opacity modifier.

This makes the timing Slider’s disabled environment and tint match the existing Image-opacity reference while preserving surrounding controls’ apparent brightness and all functional rules.

LIMITATION

SwiftUI Slider does not publicly expose separate thumb, filled-track, and unfilled-track colors. Matching the modifier hierarchy is the safest native solution, but exact pixel-identical rendering cannot be guaranteed across iPadOS 17 and newer systems without a custom/UIKit slider, which is outside scope.
