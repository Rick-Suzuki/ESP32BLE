B. ON and OFF reference geometry on iPhone

ON and OFF use the same sliderRow implementation:

• Fixed label column: 90 points.
• Label-to-content spacing: 9 points.
• No arrow buttons on iPhone.
• Slider/value column has 12-point horizontal padding on both sides.
• The two rows are grouped in a VStack, then placed as the leading content of an HStack followed by a Spacer:

Set_KB_Timing.swift:50–58

This leading-group-plus-Spacer arrangement establishes the current correct reference width.

C. REC OFF geometry on iPhone

REC OFF now correctly uses the same internal row geometry as ON/OFF:

• 90-point label column: line 147.
• 9-point HStack spacing: line 143.
• 12-point horizontal slider-column padding: line 180.
• No iPhone arrow buttons.

Therefore, the prior iPad correction from spacing 0 to spacing 9 is present and correct.

The remaining difference is its parent layout. On iPhone, REC OFF is inserted directly into the section’s outer VStack:

Set_KB_Timing.swift:30–32

It is not contained in the ON/OFF-style leading group followed by a trailing Spacer. Consequently, SwiftUI can offer its flexible Slider a different width proposal from the one received by the ON/OFF group. This is the confirmed structural cause of the remaining iPhone inconsistency.

D. IMAGE geometry on iPhone

IMAGE has two confirmed layout differences from ON/OFF:

1. Its row uses 4-point spacing rather than 9 points:

Set_KB_Timing.swift:201

This places the IMAGE slider column 5 points farther left than the timing-slider columns.

2. The Slider shares a VStack with the four cyan image controls:

Set_KB_Timing.swift:207–214

Those controls have an 18-point HStack spacing and 28-point symbols:

Set_ImgCtlsSection.swift:22–35, 56–58, 70–72

The image-controls view also declares:

.frame(maxWidth: .infinity, alignment: .leading)

at Set_ImgCtlsSection.swift:37.

This means the IMAGE slider’s available width is influenced by both its direct placement in the outer VStack and the image-control row, rather than by the ON/OFF reference grouping.

E. Confirmed causes

REC OFF:
• Its internal spacing and padding now match ON/OFF.
• Its parent containment does not: it is a direct flexible child of the outer VStack, unlike the ON/OFF leading group.

IMAGE:
• It has a 4-point gap instead of the 9-point reference gap.
• Its Slider shares a flexible column with the image controls.
• It is also a direct child of the outer VStack rather than being contained in the ON/OFF-style leading group.

There is no difference in slider binding, range, step, timing logic, persistence, or iPad arrow-button logic causing the visual width differences.

K. Smaller-iPhone risks

The proposed containment changes should reduce, not increase, the horizontal demand of REC OFF and IMAGE because the trailing Spacer absorbs unused width.

The remaining device-test risk is the intrinsic width of the four-image-control row. If it is wider than the ON/OFF reference width on a particular small landscape iPhone, a follow-up would need one measured, iPhone-only width constraint for the IMAGE content column. There is no existing fixed width constant in source that safely identifies that value without visual testing.
